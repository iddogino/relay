What Relay needs

  • A Mac with macOS 15 or later (Apple silicon), or an iPhone or iPad with iOS 18 or later.
  • A machine to work on that you can reach over SSH: a Mac with Remote Login on, a Linux server, a cloud machine. Relay isn’t a hosting service; it connects to machines you already have.
  • tmux on that machine, which keeps sessions running when you’re away. If it’s missing, Relay offers to install it for you, without needing an administrator password.

Adding a machine

Choose New Project, then Add Host, and give the machine’s address and your user name there. The first time, sign in with your password or an existing key; Relay can then set up a key of its own, kept in iCloud Keychain so your other devices sign in without asking again. Test Connection says what’s wrong when something is.

Using this Mac as a host

Turn on System Settings ▸ General ▸ Sharing ▸ Remote Login, then in Relay choose Settings ▸ This Mac ▸ Add This Mac. Sessions on this Mac then keep running after Relay quits, and you can pick them up from your iPhone or iPad when the Mac is reachable from where you are (the same network, or a VPN such as Tailscale).

A host says it “needs setup” on iPhone or iPad

That host signs in on your Mac with a key file or an SSH agent, which the phone doesn’t have. Tap Set Up… under the host and choose a Relay key in iCloud Keychain; after one sign-in, every device can use it.

Hosts and projects don’t appear on another device

Check that both devices are signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud on, and that Settings ▸ iCloud in Relay shows sync turned on. Sync Now there fetches right away.
