Privacy

Relay collects nothing. There is no Relay account, no analytics, no advertising, and no server of ours between you and your machines. This page says what the app stores, where, and who it talks to.

What we collect

Nothing. The app doesn’t send us usage data, crash reports, identifiers or anything else, and it contains no third-party analytics or advertising code. We can’t see your hosts, your sessions, your keys, or what you type.

What the app stores, and where

  • On your device: the hosts and projects you add, the host keys you’ve trusted, and your settings.
  • In your device’s Keychain: SSH keys Relay makes or imports, the passwords of hosts you sign in to with one, and your GitHub sign-in. Keys kept in the Secure Enclave never leave that device.
  • In your iCloud, if sync is on: hosts, projects and trusted host keys are kept in your private iCloud database, and keys, passwords and the GitHub sign-in in iCloud Keychain, so your other devices have them. That data is stored by Apple under your Apple Account and Apple’s privacy policy; we have no access to it. You can turn sync off in Settings ▸ iCloud, and Relay then keeps everything on the device.

Your sessions themselves run on your own machines, in tmux. Relay keeps no copy of what’s in them.

Who the app talks to

  • Your hosts, over SSH, directly from your device. Nothing is relayed through us.
  • GitHub, if you sign in: to show a session’s pull request and its checks, Relay asks GitHub’s API about the repositories your sessions are in, with the sign-in you gave it. That’s between your device and GitHub, under GitHub’s privacy statement. Sign out in Settings and the sign-in is deleted.
  • iCloud, if sync is on, as above.
  • Updates: the App Store updates the App Store version. The version downloaded from this site checks for updates by fetching a file from GitHub and downloads new versions from there; GitHub sees the request, as any web server would.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Local network, to reach hosts on the network you’re on.
  • Notifications, if you want to hear when a session needs you. They’re made on your device.
  • Files and folders, only the ones you pick: a key file, a folder like ~/.ssh, or a file you drop on a terminal to upload to your host.

This website

relayapp.sh has no analytics, no cookies and no tracking. It’s served by Cloudflare, which processes requests (including IP addresses) to deliver and protect the site. Download links go to GitHub.

Children

Relay is a developer tool. It isn’t directed at children, and collects no data from anyone.

Changes and questions

If this changes, this page will say so, with a new date above. Relay is open source, so everything here can be checked against the code. Questions: open an issue on GitHub.
